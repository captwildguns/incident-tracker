Incidents Launch Plan02
The problem we designed around

The operational truth

An incident almost never gets written up while it is happening. The driver’s job in the moment is to keep 50 kids safe and keep the bus moving. The report comes later, once they have pulled into the school loop or gotten back to the garage.
  • A driver files from a tablet after the route, while the details are still fresh
  • Every capture screen is built around that after-the-fact moment, not a live event
  • We are not showing the driver tablet today. That is a separate Tyler Drive build, same capture logic

Incidents Launch Plan04
The core idea

Every incident type carries a response path

  • A response path is the district’s policy for that kind of incident, written down as an ordered list of steps
  • A step is one action with one owning role, a time expectation, and a notification that fires when it becomes active
  • Some steps require sign-off. The next step does not open until the approval lands
  • The incident type and severity choose the path automatically. Nobody picks a workflow, and nobody assembles one by hand
Five types today because those are the five categories districts already log, and each one carries different obligations. Paths run 3 to 6 steps. Disruptive Behavior 3 · Physical Altercation 5 · Prohibited Items 5 · Safety Violation 5 · Property Damage 6

Incidents Launch Plan06
Per-student handling

One event, many students

  • Add a second student and the record splits: one real-world event, one tracked record per child
  • Each student carries a role — instigator, victim, witness, bystander — and severity can be overridden per student
  • Because the roles differ, the response path differs for each child in the same incident
INC-2025-0059 — one altercation, three students. Switch student and the banner, the severity, and the workflow all change. Chris Park, present but not involved, gets no workflow at all.
Incidents Launch Plan07
Getting work done

The guided response

  • Nobody stares at a five-step form. A coordinator sees the one step that is theirs, right now
  • Each step carries a plain-English what to do next checklist
  • Steps needing sign-off ask for approval instead of completion
  • Complete one and the baton passes, with its own notification
Workflow tab — the assigned path, the current step, and who owns it
Incidents Launch Plan08
Districts are not identical

Configurable, not hard-coded

  • Workflow builder: add, reorder, and retire steps without a code change
  • Step templates carry the notified group and the email template
  • Roles and permissions managed in-district, five groups out of the box
The biggest feedback ask of the project: do these steps match real district policy?
Workflow management — six paths today, each editable step by step
